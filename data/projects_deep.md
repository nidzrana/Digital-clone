# My projects in more detail

## MindBridge: why I built it
MindBridge is a chatbot for students who live far away from home for their studies and feel homesick. I built it because being away from family is hard for a lot of students, and a quiet, always-available chatbot can be a first place to talk and get safe guidance.

## MindBridge: my role
I built MindBridge alone. I did all of the coding and the technical work myself. My teammates did the documentation and the presentation.

## MindBridge: how it works
It is a RAG-based chatbot. It looks up relevant passages from its own knowledge base and answers from them. I compared a small local model (Flan-T5) with a Groq Llama RAG pipeline to see which gave better and safer answers. The hardest part for me was the API configuration.

## MindBridge: the five stress levels and the safety check
MindBridge sorts each message into one of 5 stress levels, and every answer goes through a safety check. If a message sounds critical, the bot does not just chat. It tells the student that urgent medical help is needed and points them towards it. In my tests the safe-response accuracy was 98%.

## MindBridge: what I would improve
With more time I would make MindBridge more intelligent. My biggest idea is to let it place an emergency call to a hospital on its own when something serious happens and medical care is needed.

## Campus Marketplace: what I built
Campus Marketplace is a mobile app with 4 user roles that works across multiple campuses. It was my first app, so it was challenging and I had to get out of my comfort zone. I built the Campus Admin portal, which is 23 of the 54 screens. As campus admin a user can approve or reject admin requests and remove fraudulent sellers from the marketplace. I used React Native, Expo and Supabase.

## Kharchay: what it is and why I built it
Kharchay is an expense tracker app I built for my Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) course. I wanted an interactive, colourful and attractive interface that is fun to use and easy enough for someone who is not well educated or not comfortable with technology. I designed it in Figma, built it in React Native, and evaluated it using Nielsen's usability heuristics.

## Campus Marketplace: the hardest part
The hardest part of Campus Marketplace was routing. I had a lot of routing problems, getting the screens and navigation between the different roles to work properly. Since it was my first app, I learned a lot by fixing them one by one.
